## Reading

From Esor Huang's article on why neither RSS nor Facebook's algorithm cures information
overload.[^esor] Whatever the tool, reading without a goal either buries you in information or
narrows your view. The answer is to read vertically: pick something you want to make, then read
what it needs while you make it.

> 我有沒有決定自己的閱讀目標是什麼？我的這個閱讀目標想要創造什麼實現？

> 不是我要怎麼讀到好資訊，而是思考我要利用資訊創造屬於我自己的什麼東西？

> 先有一個明確要實踐的目標，以實踐為目的，然後才去掌握這個領域的資訊。

> 如果沒有這個目標性的思考，筆記就會變成只是複製，而資訊閱讀也會變成過眼雲煙了。

> 無論是傳統媒體給我們報導、 RSS 閱讀器給我們訂閱的資訊、臉書給我們可能想看的消息，其實都還是缺乏目標性的「廣泛閱讀」，當然，這當作一種刺激也無不可。但若是這當作唯一的閱讀方法，就很容易變成讀了很多，但什麼都沒發生。

## Taking notes in Logseq

The community's answers to "Our Logseq mottos" on the Logseq forum:[^logseq]

- "Everything starts in the journal." (qwxlea)
- "Start writing and let the structure grow, as opposed to defining a workflow before you even
  start." (Ed Nico)
- "Tag anything you might want to come back to later." (LarryK)
- "Stop trying so hard." (random geek)
- "Read with purpose." (s0ren)
- "Write once, tag twice, and think thrice." (Sayakaya28)
- "Just get it out!" Although having thought about some upfront structure really helps you get
  it out. (Dario)
- "Start with today and tag away." (imnegan)
